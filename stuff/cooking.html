If you are this person - “I want to get into cooking, but I don’t know where to start and the investment needed is so high 🫤 At home I am not allowed to cook either 😛” - then this guide is exactly for you.

This page collects resources I have curated on the theory and practice of cooking, to help you get started (or discover new methods and vegetarian recipes) on your cooking journey! Food is very personal, essential to all life, brings people together, and quite literally - you are what you eat. In my opinion, every person should know how to cook, even if only for a few meals a week. At the very least, it’s one of the best hobbies to have.

My sources of information are:

  • Generational knowledge - recipes from my great-grandmother, passed down to my mom.
  • Recipes, techniques and methods I tested, explored and discovered through my own cooking journey.
  • Cookbooks, YouTubers, and other nutritional guides that I have curated.

I am grateful to my mom for her vast knowledge of recipes across diverse cuisines. We never get bored of the food at home - she has so many variations on common recipes. Building on her knowledge, I am exploring more cuisines, and will document what I discover on this page.

🌱

The information on this page is for vegetarian cooking. Even “hardcore meat lovers” and “foodies” know they have to reduce their meat consumption, so I hope you’ll see the value in vegetarian and vegan cooking.

So many topics to discuss!

For absolute beginners

Tips & resources

Welcome to the start of your cooking journey! Trust me, you won’t regret it.

  1. Step one

    Immersion

    As children we learn by observation. Languages are best learnt when you’re immersed in and surrounded by them - before my Hindi exams in school, I’d listen to only Hindi music the day before, to get my mind thinking in Hindi. Cooking, similarly, is a language and a form of expression. The fastest way to learn would be to shadow cooks, although they’d be annoyed :P The next best thing is to watch cooking videos. Tons of them! Search up your favourite recipes, find channels you like, and just watch. That’s how my journey began.

    In your mind, you start assembling the building blocks of the language of cooking - noting how different ingredients and foods interact with each other, with different kinds of heat, and so on.

  2. Step two

    Determination

    Be curious. Be determined, and aim to cook confidently one day. Your first few recipes and attempts may be disastrous - don’t let that shake your faith and determination. It’s all part of the learning journey. Everyone starts somewhere.

  3. Step three

    Theory

    Now that you can speak words, form sentences and express yourself through cooking, it’s time to join school - the school of cooking. Some of our parents learnt to cook purely through practice and exposure. Ask them why they add a certain ingredient and they may not be able to explain, and would get annoyed at further questions :P

    At the same time, one can get too lost in theory; for daily cooking, it’s ultimately practice that makes you efficient. But the two go hand in hand. The fastest learning comes from investing in both at once - practising, experimenting, understanding and questioning - to take your cooking to the next level.

Theory principles

Here are some principles to get you started:

  1. The effect of temperature in steaming, roasting, frying, baking, microwaving, and more.
  2. The effect of altitude (air pressure) - cooking in an open pot, with a closed lid, and in a pressure cooker.
  3. Food bases, and how they vary across cultures.
